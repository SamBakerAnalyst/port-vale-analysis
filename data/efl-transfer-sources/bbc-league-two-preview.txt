League Two never fails to provide gripping entertainment right until the final day of the season.

If recent years are anything to go by, the 2026-27 campaign promises to be no different with Salford City tipped to win promotion to League One for the first time in their history and York City back in the EFL after a 10-year hiatus.

With Saturday's curtain-raiser upon us, BBC Sport spoke to correspondents across England and Wales for their takes on the clubs they cover and what to expect this season.

## Accrington Stanley

Andy Bayes, BBC Radio Lancashire

Manager: John Doolan

Last season: 16th

Key ins: Joe Anderson (Barrow, undisclosed), Cole Stockton (Salford, free), Tom Moore (Southport, undisclosed), Louie Moulden (Norwich, loan)

Key outs: Ollie Wright (loan spell ended)

What are the expectations heading into the season? To achieve more points and a higher league position than they did last season. They made a three-point improvement year-on-year last season and flirted with the play-off places in early 2026 before injuries and squad depth took their toll.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? They have strengthened their forward line with the arrival of Cole Stockton, which has to help. Stanley will be hoping that he can return to the form that made him a wanted man during his days at Morecambe.

They have also brought players in from non-league football who will be hungry to show that they belong in the EFL. The club always has a 'never say die' attitude. They are always tipped for relegation in whichever league they play at the start of a season. That's of no interest to manager John Doolan or his squad.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? Injuries and perhaps in some areas a little bit of inexperience could hamper them.

There are key players in the Stanley team that Doolan needs to get 35-plus games from this season - Shaun Whalley being a case in point. He missed the start of last season and was missed badly.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? Shaun Whalley. Stanley's results with Whalley in the side are far different to when he isn't. He's a vastly experienced, talented player who his team-mates respect greatly. If he can stay fit for most of the season, they'll be fine.

## Barnet

Emma Jones, BBC Radio London

Manager: Dean Brennan

Last season: 8th

Key ins: Oisin Gallagher (Lincoln City, loan), Matthew Cox (Brentford, loan)

Key outs: Ryan Glover (Stockport County, undisclosed), Callum Stead (Cambridge United, undisclosed)

What are the expectations heading into the season? Barnet were back in the EFL for the first time in seven years last season and finished eighth, just outside the play-off places. So minimum expectation is the play-offs, although the Bees have never been promoted via that route.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? The manager, Dean Brennan. No matter what the changes or challenges, he never leaves anyone in doubt about his aims or expectations. Plus defensive rock and leader Danny 'Mr Dependable' Collinge.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? The loss of some big players, plenty of new signings, and the need for those new additions to gel quickly with the team. Barnet have one of the smallest budgets, so Brennan's knowledge of non-league is crucial.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? Tenacious, highly-skilled pass master Nnamdi Ofoborh is the conductor. Idris Kanu is the creator. Academy graduate Bright Siaw looks an exciting prospect.

## Bristol Rovers

Shutterstock

Steve Evans has won 14 of his 26 games in charge of Bristol Rovers since taking over as boss in December 2025

Richard Hoskin, BBC Radio Bristol

Manager: Steve Evans

Last season: 14th

Key ins: Kofi Balmer (Motherwell, free), Bobby Kamwa (Newport County, undisclosed), Reiss-Alexander Russell-Denny (Tottenham, loan), Tommy Leigh (Bradford City, loan), Richie Smallwood (Tranmere Rovers, free)

Key outs: Isaac Hutchinson (Doncaster Rovers, undisclosed), Kamil Conteh (Bromley, undisclosed)

What are the expectations heading into the season? Rovers ended last season so strongly under Steve Evans that supporters will be hoping to carry that momentum into the new campaign.

It's not gone unnoticed that a number of national pundits and publications are tipping Rovers for automatic promotion – and while a top-three finish is the expectation of most, experienced supporters will want the season to settle down before making any committed predictions.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? A number of players drafted in last January have returned to the club – Kofi Balmer, Tommy Leigh and Richie Smallwood. Defensively Rovers are strong, with a lot of players boasting previous promotions on their CVs.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? There's nothing drastically weak about Bristol Rovers. Some supporters have pointed out that the squad would benefit from the addition of a wide man, but that could well be addressed by the end of the transfer window.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? I've yet to see him play – but recent arrival Reiss-Alexander Russell-Denny could be a key addition to the squad. He'll be keen to emulate the impact of Yusuf Akhamrich, who had a successful loan spell from Tottenham last season.

## Cheltenham Town

Steve Kitchen, BBC Radio Gloucestershire

Manager: Steve Cotterill

Last season: 18th

Key ins: Andreas Weimann - among a number of players with higher league experience.

Key outs: Bristol Rovers loanee - Isaac Hutchinson. He scored 15 goals in League Two, finishing with 18 goals in all competitions.

What are the expectations heading into the season? To build on the foundations that manager Steve Cotterill put in place last season. The 2025-26 campaign was about survival, with 18th place a fantastic achievement in the end. Many fans are talking about top half, with a shot at the play-offs if things click.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? After returning to Whaddon Road, with the club favourites for relegation last season, Cotterill reshaped the squad with contacts and hard work on the training ground. Cotterill brought the previously bewildered fans back on side, trusting and backing the manager and his players. That work continues this season with what looks to be a stronger, deeper squad.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? Until the signing of Andreas Weimann, there was a lack of attacking experience alongside an exciting group of younger players looking to learn and strengthen their careers. Cheltenham will need them to settle quickly to life in League Two whilst finding the net, but it now looks like a well balanced squad on the available budget.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? It's easy to say Weimann but in reality there is no 'one player'. The key to Cheltenham's success will be Cotterill and tune that he gets out of a group of players that have joined the club because they've either played for him before, or have been enthused by what he's sold them.

From the kit design to the nets in the goals, he's involved in everything that happens at the club. It's no accident that he was (long overdue) given the keys to the Borough at the end of last season.

## Chesterfield

Paul Fisher, BBC Radio Sheffield

Manager: Paul Cook

Last season: 6th (lost in play-off semi-finals)

Key ins: Junior Eccleston (Sutton United, undisclosed), George McEachran (Grimsby Town, free), Jack Simpson (Leyton Orient, free), Alfie Pond (Wolverhampton Wanderers, free), Remeao Hutton (Gillingham, free), Fraser Bryden (Crusaders, undisclosed)

Key outs: Bailey Hobson (Harrogate Town, free), Armando Dobra (Leyton Orient, undisclosed), Jamie Grimes (Boston United, free), Janoi Donacien (Northampton Town, free)

What are the expectations heading into the season? Paul Cook has improved the Spireites in every single season he has managed the club, including the first time he was at Chesterfield just under 14 years ago.

Last season was disappointing that they could not reach the top three despite being tipped by many. This time, Cook and the fans expect promotion.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? Defensively they looked to have strengthened, adding youth to experience, and pace up front will also hopefully be to their benefit.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? Chesterfield conceded the most goals of any team in the top eight last season and still reached the play-offs. If they shore up at the back, top three will be achievable, injuries apart.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? Liam Mandeville led Chesterfield's overall assists last season and was second in the table for League Two. If he's on song, then he could do it again.

## Colchester United

Victoria Polley, BBC Essex

Manager: Danny Cowley

Last season: 12th

Key ins: Sean Raggett (Rotherham, free), Thimothee Lo-Tutala (Hull City, loan), Bradley Ihionvien (Peterborough United, laon)

Key outs: Kyreece Lisbie (Reading, undisclosed), Arthur Read (York City, free), Matt Macey (Wycombe Wanderers, free)

What are the expectations heading into the season? It's difficult to know exactly what to expect from Colchester United this season. The previous campaign ended with anticipation of a takeover being completed over the summer, but those plans collapsed in June.

Chairman and owner Robbie Cowling insists a competitive budget was always in place should the sale not happen, but it's not the renewed investment many supporters had hoped for.

Even so, Danny and Nicky Cowley have guided the U's to the fringes of the League Two play-off places in each of the past two seasons and have consistently recruited smartly within their means. If they can continue that trend, Colchester should again be capable of challenging in the top half and pushing for a play-off spot.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? Colchester's summer recruitment looks sensible and solid. Sean Raggett and Paul Digby bring experience, leadership and physicality, while goalkeeper Thimothee Lo-Tutala arrives after a season playing League One football with Doncaster Rovers.

Perhaps more importantly, the U's have retained several key players. Highly rated left-back Ellis Iandolo has committed his future to the club, while influential midfielder Jack Payne remains. Last season's standout performers Jack Tucker and Harry Anderson have also stayed put, giving the squad valuable continuity.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? The biggest concern is a loss of pace and attacking threat in wide areas. Kyreece Lisbie's sale to Reading, coupled with the departures of Owura Edwards and Samson Tovide following the expiry of their contracts, has left gaps to fill.

Former players Bradley Ihionvien and Oscar Thorn have returned on loan deals, providing some familiarity and attacking options. However, Colchester will need to find goals from within the squad quickly if they are to mount a sustained challenge near the top end of the table.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? Jack Payne remains Colchester's chief creative force. Operating in the number 10 role, he scored 13 goals last season, including two hat-tricks, and continues to be one of the most effective attacking midfielders in League Two.

## Crawley Town

Gary Smith, reporter, BBC Radio Sussex

Manager: Colin Kazim-Richards

Last season: 22nd

Key ins: Vito Mannone, Jude Arthurs (Bromley, free transfer), Stephen Duke-McKenna (free transfer)

Key outs: Jacob Chapman (return from loan spell), Max Anderson (released), Josh Flint (released), Harry Forster (released), Harry McKirdy (released)

What are the expectations heading into the season? Most people will have Crawley listed as relegation favourites once again and after a relegation and a near relegation in the past two seasons, most fans will be hoping that the coming season is far better.

Having seen one pre-season friendly plus the game at Watford last week, my own expectations are that the side will surprise plenty of people and will comfortably finish mid-table or above.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? The strengths that will help them achieve that is that the head coach now has a squad to work with that he has put together himself as opposed to a squad that he inherited for the final seven games of last season. He now has the chance to impose his own coaching methods and style of play on the side.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? The relatively small budget that Crawley have in comparison to other sides could be the thing that holds them back. Having a whole new squad could be a great thing as mentioned but it also takes time for a squad to gel so hopefully this won't be a factor that prevents them getting off to a flying start in League Two.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? Jude Arthurs. It won't be for his goalscoring prowess but more for his ability to knit the play together from his position as a defensive midfielder.

Pemi Aderoju who is on a season long loan from Peterborough could also a player to look out for. His off the ball running and his ability to get beyond the defence coupled with great pace could be the key to unlocking many defences.

## Crewe Alexandra

Graham McGarry, BBC Radio Stoke

Manager: Lee Bell

Last season: 11th

Key ins: Luke Offord (MK Dons, free), Joe White (Newcastle United, undisclosed), Jordan Gibson (Doncaster Rovers, free)

Key outs: Max Sanders (Notts County, free), Jack Powell (Hornchurch, free), Shilow Tracey (released), Louis Moult (Telford, free), Omar Bogle (released), Charlie Finney (released), James Connolly (Tranmere Rovers, free)

What are the expectations heading into the season? Crewe will have to defy the odds if they are to improve on last season and feature in what looks like being a competitive promotion race.

Rumours of a possible takeover in what will be the club's 150th anniversary have gathered momentum throughout the close season, but as yet nothing has been finalised, which will mean manager Lee Bell again having to operate with one of the smallest budgets in the division.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? While the recruitment of players will be limited, Bell and his staff look to have made some early positive signings which have been welcomed by supporters.

If the manager can keep most of his squad fit, which has not happened for the past two seasons, then there is no reason why Crewe cannot be dark horses to achieve promotion.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? The lack of strength in depth could be a problem, especially if injuries continue to hamper team selection.

Crewe need to show a marked improvement on last season's away form, which brought 11 defeats and only seven wins. At least another dozen points are needed if the Railwaymen are to be serious contenders for a top-seven finish.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? All the summer signings have brought some real quality to the squad, but if homegrown player Joel Tabiner can return with all guns blazing following his horrendous ACL injury that kept him sidelined for 12 months, he will make Crewe a stronger squad. The return of Tabiner will bring more creativity and goals.

## Fleetwood Town

Phil Gittins, BBC Radio Lancashire

Manager: Matt Lawlor

Last season: 15th

Key ins: Danny Andrew (free), Jake Batty (free), Andy Cannon (free), Kane Thompson-Sommers (MK Dons, undisclosed), Josh Thomas (free)

Key outs: James Bolton (released), Elliott Bonds (Salford City, undisclosed), Harrison Holgate, Zech Medley (York City, free), James Norwood (released), Matty Virtue (released)

What are the expectations heading into the season? This is a difficult one to answer. The club have been clear that finance is not at the level it has been in previous years. The importance of a rich supply line from the highly respected academy to the first team has never been greater.

Recruitment over the summer brought both experience and potential. How quickly and well they blend will be crucial in head coach Matt Lawlor's first full season at the helm.

Difficult trips to promotion hopefuls Chesterfield and Grimsby await in August and will provide a better indication of where the Cod Army are at. It's very much a case of watch this space...

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? Danny Andrew has returned for his third spell and alongside established squad members Ched Evans, Shaun Rooney and Jay Lynch, offers a depth of experience and know-how - vital commodities for all teams who are successful at this level.

Allied to those qualities, they are all well versed in the standards and expectations of the football club, which will be helpful to their youthful head coach as he looks to instil the right values into the younger elements of his squad.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? Perhaps the biggest flaw within the make-up of the current squad is their frustrating propensity to ship late goals at the business end of games.

On nine occasions last season such lapses in concentration saw Fleetwood concede in the final 10 minutes. Worryingly this trait has already reared its head again in pre-season.

The club's progress will continue to be blighted until this trend is addressed, something that Lawlor is acutely aware of.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? Having finished last season as the club's leading scorer, it would be easy and wholly accurate to identify Mark Helm as a key man for Fleetwood this campaign.

However, in an era when the importance of academy progression to the first team squad has never been more important, watch out for Crispin McLean - a young and talented midfielder, highly regarded by Lawlor, who to date has managed two goals from his seven first team appearances.

## Gillingham

Matt Cole, BBC Radio Kent

Manager: Gareth Ainsworth

Last season: 17th

Key ins: Liam Gordon (Motherwell, free), James Brophy (Cambridge United, free), Will Goodwin (Oxford United, free), Ollie Wright (Southampton, loan)

Key outs: Sam Gale (Barnsley, undisclosed), Remeao Hutton (Chesterfield, free), Josh Andrews (Grimsby Town, free)

What are the expectations heading into the season? Back-to-back 17th-place finishes in League Two have certainly tempered the optimism of all but the most positive supporters.

The Gills' recent habit of starting seasons like they're going to win the league, then finishing with a whimper, also feeding a sense of pre-season caution around Priestfield.

Gareth Ainsworth has been given license by the owners, not only to stay on for a second season, but also to make upwards of a dozen new signings. Gillingham's success relies on what he's dubbed "his team" and "his era."

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? A mix of youth and experience - particularly in the new signings - could be the key. Scottish defender Liam Gordon looks just the sort of leader they've lacked at the back.

At the other end of the age spectrum, if defender Connor McManus, midfielder Zane Albarus and winger Sheldon Marshall make good on their considerable promise, it could be the season Gills' fans have (long) hoped for.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? Having reached a club record 21-game unbeaten run in September, Gills' season fell apart.

In truth, they were weak in all departments. Fans and the manager are hoping that shedding 17 players over the summer, along with the new additions, will turn a decent looking side on paper into one that can take them into the top half and maybe higher.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? January acquisition Ronan Hale arrived for a substantial fee and with a prolific scoring record in Scotland and Northern Ireland.

If this new-look Gills squad can provide him chances, he could help turn them from underachievers to a real threat in League Two.

## Grimsby Town

Matt Dean, BBC Radio Humberside

What are the expectations heading into the season? David Artell has improved the club each season that he's been at Blundell Park. After losing in last season's play-off semi-final to Salford, it would be a disappointment if the team didn't finish in at the least the top seven this season.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? Grimsby were among the highest creator of chances and goals scored in League Two last season.

Their possession-based brand of football and philosophy of spreading the goalscoring responsibilities throughout the team might help make them a significant force again this season.

## Newport County

Can the likes of Christian Doidge, Shaquille Gwengwe and Yahya Bamba deliver in League Two? A lack of pace in wide areas is another concern.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? Biggins may have been discarded by Shrewsbury, but he should be the midfield lynchpin County lacked for half of last season.

## Northampton Town

Jake Sharpe, BBC Radio Northampton

Manager: Chris Hogg

Last season: 24th in League One (relegated)

Key ins: Matty Warhurst (Manchester City, undisclosed), Janoi Donacien (Chesterfield, free), Connor Evans (MK Dons, free), Lee Evans (Bradford City, free)

Key outs: Cameron McGeehan (Barnsley, free), Nesta Guinness-Walker (Aberdeen, free), Dean Campbell (Notts County, undisclosed)

What are the expectations heading into the season? It's difficult to say. Northampton Town are usually a big fish pushing for promotion in League Two. But the scale of the collapse of last season in which they lost their last 12 competitive games means a year of consolidation may be required.

Couple that with an exciting, albeit inexperienced, managerial appoint with Chris Hogg taking the reins in his first managerial role, anything mid-table upwards would be a success.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? The manager. He has a huge coaching pedigree which has been evident in how he has set this team up in pre-season.

There is fair amount of experience in the side as well. Jon Guthrie and Elliott Moore in the heart of defence are stalwarts at this level. Sam Hoskins is entering his 12th season with the club.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? Final third quality. They have invested in striker Matty Warhurst who has shown his predatory instincts in pre-season.

But he needs a consistent source of supply and the supporting cast have yet to prove they're up to it. There is still time for a few more additions in those areas.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? Again I'm going for Warhurst. It's a big call for a player with one senior appearance to his name. But very rarely do the Cobblers sign this profile of player and I've seen enough in pre-season to suggest he is capable of scoring goals at this level if he gets the opportunities.

Finding a goalscorer is the ultimate challenge and if Northampton Town have indeed found one then it could be the difference between success and mediocrity.

## Oldham Athletic

He helped Notts County navigate their way to League One last time around. Maybe he can do the same this year at Boundary Park.

## Port Vale

Getty Images

New Port Vale midfielder Kyle Dempsey made 29 appearances in League One last season to help Bolton Wanderers win promotion to the Championship

Phil Bowers, BBC Radio Stoke

Manager: Jon Brady

Last season: 22nd in League One (relegated)

Key ins: Kyle Dempsey (Bolton Wanderers, free), Jasper Moon (Burton Albion, free), Jackson Smith (Barnsley, undisclosed)

Key outs: Jayden Stockley (AFC Wimbledon, free), Mitch Clark (Swindon Town, free), Ruari Paton (St Johnstone, undisclosed)

What are the expectations heading into the season? Twofold: Promotion and restore some faith in the team. Last season started with plenty of high hopes and ended with a relegation Jon Brady couldn't avoid despite improving performances as the season drew to a close.

After promotion campaigns in their past two seasons at this level, a top-seven finish is what most fans expect – especially given the investment in both the pitch and the playing squad.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? Midfield and defence look strong. Both new goalkeepers in Jackson Smith and Mason Terry arrive with glowing reputations, while Jasper Moon, Lewis Montsma and Aaron McGowan reinforce a backline already featuring promotion winners Kyle John and Connor Hall.

Further up the pitch, adding Bolton's Kyle Dempsey to George Byers, Ben Garrity and Rhys Walters looks impressive business. Onel Hernandez's magic on the wing should also catch the eye.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? It's when you get to the final third that the reservations start to emerge about automatic promotion.

Mo Faal had an unproductive loan spell last season (but did arrive injured and without a pre-season) so questions have been asked about bringing him back. Oli Lynch is unproven at EFL level, Max Watters' career has stalled after a prolific spell at Crawley six years ago and Tyreece Simpson has been beset by injury problems.

Brady has pointed to bringing goals from other areas of the team, but fans are wary of the strikers' ability to score enough to secure promotion.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? A half-fit Onel Hernandez looked a class above at times last season, so when he proved his fitness in the summer to sign a 12-month contract, his quick feet, trickery and bravery on the ball should provide some entertaining football to get fans off their seats.

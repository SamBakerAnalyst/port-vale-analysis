League One has some titans joining the fray this season.

Leicester City lifted the Premier League trophy a decade ago but now they return to the third tier for the first time since 2009, while Sheffield Wednesday are back to kick off a new era under brand new ownership.

But with such recent, rich history comes mammoth expectation and for the Foxes in particular, nothing else will do other than promotion.

While for a club like last season's League Two champions Bromley, who have already reached a new height in securing promotion to League One for the first time, the pressure is off.

Could this be another year of the underdogs?

Follow your League One club this 2026-27 season

## AFC Wimbledon

Aaron Paul, BBC Radio 5 Live

Key ins: Dan Sweeney (Stevenage, free), Steve Seddon re-signing for the new season

Key outs: Omar Bugiel (Suzhou Dongwu, free)

What are the expectations heading into the season? Keep their heads above water, and keep improving.

What are the major strengths of the team to help them achieve that? Defensively the Dons can be tough to breakdown.

And what are the underlying weaknesses that could hold them back? Far too streaky, runs of wins will almost certainly be followed by a long barren drought.

Who's a player to look out for this season who could be a difference-maker? Marcus Browne - technically brilliant, he's a key man in all good things they do.

## Barnsley

Getty Images

Daniel Stendel was reappointed Barnsley boss in May after Conor Hourihane stepped down by mutual consent

Adam Oxley, BBC Radio Sheffield

Head coach: Daniel Stendel

Last season: 15th

Key ins: Cameron McGeehan (Northampton, free), Sam Gale (Gillingham, undisclosed), Clayton Taylor (Newcastle Jets, undisclosed)

Key outs: David McGoldrick (Mansfield, free), Adam Phillips (Bradford, undisclosed), Eoghan O'Connell (Stockport, free)

Expectations? Daniel Stendel

has been a popular choice, external to replace Conor Hourihane as head coach this summer. The German has returned to Oakwell hoping to repeat the success he achieved in his first spell when he guided the Reds to the Championship in 2018-19.

Promotion feels a long shot.

Barnsley have a limited budget as the owners aim to operate within their means. A top-half finish would feel like a step in the right direction for the Reds, but the fanbase - who have seen their side play in the second tier for the majority of the club's history - will expect the team to be at least knocking on the door of the play-offs.

Strengths? The focus has been on recruiting young, hungry players to Stendel's high-energy, high-pressing game.

Retaining key midfielders in Patrick Kelly, Luca Connell and Reyes Cleary, coupled with the youthful enthusiasm of the club's new signings, the majority of whom are aged 23 and under, will offer hope that Stendel can mould a team to at least cause problems for the more fancied teams in the division.

Weaknesses? The Reds have lost goals and experience this summer with last season's top three scorers David McGoldrick (18), Davis Keillor-Dunn (16) and Adam Phillips (11) all having departed Oakwell.

Returning midfielder Cameron McGeehan, who previously played at Oakwell in Stendel's first spell, is currently the only player in his 30s in the squad, and a lack of a few experienced heads has historically not served Barnsley well.

Difference-maker? Patrick Kelly is likely to be crucial to any success that Barnsley achieve this year. The 21-year-old got better and better throughout his first campaign with the Reds and ended the season as a full Northern Ireland international, scoring his first goal for his country against France just before this summer's World Cup.

## Blackpool

Andy Bayes, BBC Radio Lancashire

Manager: Ian Evatt

Last season: 13th

Key ins: Jordan Williams (Portsmouth, loan), Dion Charles (Huddersfield, loan), Ilmari Niskanen (Exeter, free), Karoy Anderson (Charlton, undisclosed), Finley Munroe (Middlesbrough, loan)

Key outs: Ashley Fletcher (Huddersfield, free), James Husband (Doncaster, free)

Expectations? To build on the impressive end to last season, when they climbed away from trouble with six wins from their last seven games. If they can start brightly, expectations may begin to rise, although Ian Evatt is at pains to stress that Blackpool have no right to consider themselves challengers simply because of the size of the club.

Strengths? There are plenty of attacking options, but keeping them fit is an issue straight away. They've got great options in midfield, with Albie Morgan back in action after a long absence to complement the talent already there. Signing Karoy Anderson permanently is a big boost. He impressed last season while on loan.

Weaknesses? Injuries. The centre of defence has taken a big hit, with Fraser Horsfall and Michael Ihiekwe both missing the start of the campaign following injuries sustained last term. Likewise, the absence of Dale Taylor in the forward line with a hamstring problem is a huge blow.

Blackpool missed far too many players last season, and this year doesn't look much different.

Difference-maker? Josh Bowler. On his day, he is a Championship-level player, but Blackpool now need a level of consistency from him. In a team that plays three at the back, it can be difficult to get the very best out of an out-and-out wide player, but his goal on the opening day at Grimsby will no doubt give him the confidence to find more.

## Bradford City

Jamie Raynor, BBC Radio Leeds

Manager: Graham Alexander

Last season: Fourth

Key ins: Jake Beesley (Burton Albion, undisclosed), Adam Phillips (Barnsley, undisclosed), Macaulay Gillesphey (Charlton Athletic, undisclosed), Callum Connolly (Stockport County, free), Jon McCracken (Dundee, free)

Key outs: Jenson Metcalfe (Millwall, undisclosed), Max Power (Wigan Athletic, undisclosed), Sam Walker (Watford, undisclosed), Curtis Tilt (Burton Albion, undisclosed)

Expectations? It will take some going to replicate Bradford City's fourth placed finish in League One of last season, particularly given it was their first time back at the level in six years.

The Bantams proved last season that money isn't everything, but with some financial heavyweights now in the division, a successive top six finish may be an unfair expectation for this evolving club. Just outside it may still be considered a 'good' campaign.

Strengths? Recruitment has long been a strength of the football club's rapid rise over the last couple of years. That again could prove dividends from their summer work so far. Their forward line, in particular, looks well stocked with the newly acquired Jake Beesley and Adam Phillps. Add into the mix their impressive home form over that period, Valley Parade could once again prove fortress-like.

Weaknesses? Midfield remains a concern for many supporters. The heartbeat of last season's play-off push was quickly ripped apart with the sizeable sale of Jenson Metcalfe and the surprise departure of captain Max Power.

Whilst the addition of Callum Connolly offers some solution, along with the deeper deployment of new captain Antoni Sarcevic, the Bantams still feel short in the centre-point of their spine.

Difference-maker? One of their own, Bobby Pointon. The ever-flourishing homegrown talent has taken to League One like a duck to water. Still only 22, his goal return has increased season-on-season. We're yet to truly know his ceiling, but his floor continues to rise.

## Bromley

Getty Images

Bromley striker Michael Cheek (right) has never player in League One but has been a prolific goalscorer in lower divisions

Ryan Matthews, BBC Radio London

Manager: Andy Woodman

Last season: League Two champions

Key ins: Shamal George (Wycombe, free), Kamil Conteh (Bristol Rovers, undisclosed), Jacob Mendy (Peterborough, free), Chanse Headman (Harrogate Town, undisclosed), Deon Woodman (Wealdstone, undisclosed), Victor Adeboyejo (Mansfield, free), Ethon Archer (Luton Town, loan), Richard Taylor (Bolton Wanderers, loan), JJ McKiernan (Lincoln City, undisclosed)

Key outs: Idris Odutayo (Dundee, free), Jude Arthurs (Crawley Town, free), Ben Krauhaus (Brentford, end of loan), Byron Webster (retired), Carl Jenkinson (released), Kyle Cameron (Newport County, free), Sam Long (Salford City, free), Marcus Dinanga (Aldershot Town), Brooklyn Ilunga (Hartlepool Town, free)

Expectations? It is very tricky to define any level of expectations for Bromley this season. Even the most optimistic of Bromley fans wouldn't have dreamed of reaching League One so quickly, so expectations will have to be reasonable - particularly with the level of financial muscle in the league.

A good season for Bromley would be proving that their approach works at the level, with a couple of notable scalps along the way and retaining their place in the division.

Strengths? Bromley are, and have been defined by their superior fitness and robust approach in the last few seasons. Whilst they are no slouches tactically and can outplay teams on their day, Andy Woodman has built a brand of football that is centred around peak physical performance, tall imposing players and set piece excellence.

This, coupled with the incredible team spirit that Woodman has engendered at Bromley will stand them in good stead in the upcoming season.

Weaknesses? A large percentage of the squad are still unproven and untested at this level and whilst the club have moved to address this with their summer recruitment, there is still a question mark as to how they will be able to adapt to League One level.

Specifically, a lack of quality in depth particularly in forward areas could be a limiting factor - club talisman Michael Cheek has confounded expectations in League Two and, whilst you wouldn't bet against him transferring his goalscoring exploits up another level, the burden will have to be shared around.

Difference-maker? Ethon Archer - young winger on loan from Luton Town. Skilful dead ball specialist, League One experience scoring 11 goals in 81 appearances for Cheltenham, three in 23 on loan with Port Vale last season.

## Burton Albion

Dave Fletcher, BBC Radio Derby

Head coach: Gary Bowyer

Last season: 17th

Key ins: Curtis Tilt (Bradford, undisclosed), Matty Dennis (Notts County, undisclosed), Corey Addai (Stockport, loan), Gbemi Arubi (Dundalk, undisclosed)

Key outs: Jake Beedley (Bradford, undisclosed), Udoka Godwin-Malife (Reading, undisclosed), Dylan Williams (Motherwell, undisclosed), Terence Vancooten (Stevenage, undisclosed)

Expectations? Even the most optimistic Burton Albion supporter would struggle to make a case for anything other than yet another battle against relegation – and there are few out there when making their season's predictions who have the Brewers anywhere other than the bottom four.

Gary Bowyer masterminded the Great Escape in 2024-25, after arriving at the club when nobody gave them a chance of avoiding the drop. Given the summer the Brewers have had so far, staying up this time around would be the Greatest Escape.

Weaknesses? The major issue Bowyer has is, once again, the size of his squad.

Sixteen of last season's contracted players have been sold or released – including skipper Udoka Godwin-Malife and leading scorer Jake Beesley - and the three loan players who finished the campaign have departed. At the time of writing, just four have been brought in, three on permanent deals and one on loan. The club is going to have to work hard before transfer deadline day to make up the deficit.

Difference-maker? Bowyer gave a categorical 'no' when asked if he expects to have striker Tyreece Shade in his squad come the beginning of September. Charlie Webster's recent injury record might mean he remains until January at least.

Experience is an issue, so the spotlight will be on skipper Curtis Tilt and midfielder George Evans who, between them, account for the vast majority of EFL appearances in the squad as it currently stands.

## Cambridge United

Shayne Lavery (centre) registering the most efficient strike rate in League Two last season at 0.66 goals per 90 minutes

Mark Johnson, BBC Radio Cambridge

Manager: Neil Harris

Last season: Third in League Two

Key ins: Gassan Ahadme (Charlton, loan), Callum Perry (Coventry, loan), Isaac Heath (Everton, free)

Key outs: Adam Mayor (end of loan), James Gibbons (Notts County, free)

Expectations? Probably mid-table, although whether it's a comfortable or nervy mid-table remains to be seen. For what it's worth, I suspect the former.

Strengths? Head coach Neil Harris knows the division well and can set up a team with the right shape, mentality and flexibility to surprise a few in the coming season... and he'll relish the role of the underdog.

Shayne Lavery was re-signed after being released at the end of the last season; his return will bring potency and mobility to the front line, but Cambridge will need to wrap him in cotton wool to ensure they can get enough minutes from his beleaguered hamstrings.

Weaknesses? This biggest worry is the depth of the squad. The business they've been able to do so far has been excellent, but signings seem to have dried up at the moment.

This is a concern as several members of the squad will need to have their playing time carefully managed as they are far from bullet proof in terms of injury records. It feels like the squad is about three or four players light, so there's still plenty of work to be done before the transfer window closes.

Difference-maker? Gassan Ahadme is the obvious choice as he's traditional centre forward the club lacked last season (although they did pretty well finding a work-around!).

But for me, Ben Knight has the potential to be one of the most inventive players not just at the club, but in the division.

## Huddersfield Town

Paul Ogden, BBC Radio Leeds

Head coach: Martin Drury

Last season: Ninth

Key ins: Ashley Fletcher (Blackpool, free), Ibane Bowat (Portsmouth, undisclosed), Ethan Brierley (Brentford, undisclosed), Matt Young (Sunderland, loan), Derens Fernandes (Excelsior, undisclosed) and Ilias Bronkhorst (Excelsior, free)

Key outs: Alfie May (Doncaster Rovers, loan), Lee Nicholls (Preston, undisclosed),

Expectations? Ideally, an uninterrupted third successive tilt at promotion, this time without yet another change of manager/head coach in the new year. There has been a notable aim expressed by club decision-makers for the team to prioritise entertainment and avoid predictability.

Strengths? The sheer volume of brand new and as-new recruits (many of whom remain from last summer's staggering intake) over the past 14 months as a whole. The squad appears overstocked in certain positions, so departures should still be expected in the opening weeks.

Weaknesses? Individual talent is unmistakeable, but consistent collaboration has been thin at Huddersfield since relegation from the Championship.

With such an oversized player pool to start with, non-selections will be a long list, so squad harmony might be unusually hard to achieve. To manage such hazards, the addition of Alan Knill's calm experience to the dynamic younger coaching talents of Drury, Jonathan Stead and Co. is a clever strategy.

Difference-maker? For on-the-ball thrills, pacy new winger Derensili Fernandes will be hard to beat. Centre-back Jack Whatmough's recovery from being injured for most of last season could be the essential missing barricade, though much is also expected from Ibane Bowat, signed from Portsmouth.

## Leicester City

Getty Images

Caleb Okoli scored Leicester's only goal in their 1-0 win against Northampton Town in the EFL Cup at the weekend

Jack Rafferty, BBC Radio Leicester

Manager: Russell Martin

Last season: 23rd in the Championship, relegated

Key ins: Liam Cullen (Swansea, undisclosed), Conor Chaplin (Ipswich, free), Wes Burns (Ipswich, undiscosed), Tommy Watson (Brighton, loan), Admir Bristic (Olimpija Ljubljana, undisclosed)

Key outs: Abdul Fatawu (Ipswich, £20m), Ricardo Pereira (released), Harry Winks (Cagliari, undisclosed), Jordan Ayew (released), Jannik Vestergaard (released), Patson Daka (Hamburg, free)

Expectations? Anything other than an immediate return to the Championship will be considered a failure, ideally as champions.

Given they won the league in their only other season in the third tier, finishing second or below would mark the club's lowest ever league finish. With the squad Russell Martin has assembled, along with some really exciting academy talent, the Blue Army will be hoping for a very successful campaign.

Strengths? Martin appears to have had a really positive transfer window so far. As well as allowing plenty of players to depart, he has added some real talent to his ranks, including a number of players he's worked with before. Players that he trusts and who know his style well.

The club were desperately in need of a striker, and finally landed Bosnian Admir Bristic, on Tuesday. Those additions, combined with some really exciting academy talent, could produce some magic on the pitch.

Weaknesses? The transfer window isn't quite shut yet, which will concern some City fans. There are still plenty of rumours about potential departures, including centre back Harry Souttar, who captained the side in the EFL Cup last week.

Given they've already lost Ben Nelson to an ACL injury, losing another defender without a suitable replacement could leave them very short at the back.

Difference-maker? The obvious answer would be to name one of the new recruits, such as Cullen, Burns or Chaplin who have proven pedigree at this level. The player I'm most excited about is academy product Sammy Braybrooke.

After a couple of successful loans in League Two last season, the lad dubbed the 'Leicester Iniesta' looks more than ready to step up and be a key part of City's promotion push. He, alongside young right back Jayden Joseph, have been the standout players at the end of pre-season, particularly as he made his first senior start for the club in the EFL Cup.

## MK Dons

Paul Dixon, BBC Radio 3CR

Manager: Paul Warne

Last season: Second in League Two (promoted)

Key ins: Ryan Wintle (Cardiff, free), Sam Nombe (Rotherham, free), Charlie Goode (Stevenage, free)

Key outs: Luke Offord (Crewe Alexandra, free), Aaron Nemane (Rotherham, free)

Expectations? The aim is promotion, the expectation is certainly to be in and around the play-off places come the end of season shake up.

## Mansfield Town

Jake Garner, BBC Radio Nottingham

Manager: Nigel Clough

Last season: 10th

Key ins: David McGoldrick (Barnsley, free), Michael Smith (Preston North End, free), Liam Thompson (Derby County, undisclosed)

Key outs: Will Evans (Plymouth Argyle, undisclosed), Aaron Lewis (released)

Expectations? Steady progress remains the aim as Mansfield Town prepare for their third season in League One.

Last season's 10th place finish proved they can compete comfortably at this level, and an improved league position, coupled with a good cup run, would represent another successful campaign. A push for the play-offs is not beyond the realms of possibility either!

Strengths? Mansfield have strengthened their attack with the arrivals of proven goalscorers David McGoldrick and Michael Smith, helping address an issue that cost them valuable points last season.

The squad's versatility is also a major asset, with players such as Jordan Bowery and Lucas Akins capable of filling a variety of roles. Bowery especially will probably cover six different roles between now and May.

Weaknesses? The biggest concern is the age profile of the squad and whether key players can stay fit over a long season. Injuries have been a recurring problem for Mansfield in recent years, and several important figures are now in their mid-to-late thirties.

While they still bring a huge amount of quality and experience, there are understandable questions over how they will cope with the demands of another lengthy campaign. That said, age is increasingly just a number in modern football, with McGoldrick's 18 League One goals for Barnsley last season highlighting exactly that.

Difference-maker? McGoldrick will naturally attract plenty of attention, as will goalkeeper Liam Roberts, but if he stays injury free, Tyler Roberts could be the player who truly makes Mansfield tick.

His quality, agility and creativity made an immediate impact after arriving at the club last season, he looks revitalised and he's rediscovered his love for football here. A clever pass, a moment of skill or a clinical finish, there will be plenty of magical Roberts moments as he has the ability and confidence to unlock defences. Very excited to see more of him this season.

## Notts County

Adam Hassell, BBC Radio Nottingham

Head coach: Martin Paterson

Last season: Fifth in League Two (promoted via the play-offs)

Key ins: Cal Roberts (Scunthorpe, free), Jack Evans (Harrogate, undisclosed), Ollie Cooper (Swansea, loan)

Key outs: Alassana Jatta (Casa Pia, undisclosed), Matty Dennis (Burton, undisclosed), Tom Iorpenda (end of loan)

Expectations? Notts are back in the third tier for the first time since 2015 and fans will largely be happy with simply staying in the division by the end of the season.

Notts became a very workmanlike team under Martin Paterson last campaign, with individuals arguably largely playing above their abilities; the Magpies will need the same again if they're to compete with some of the big-hitters in League One.

Strengths? Martin Paterson knows how to make his teams hard to beat. They're resilient and dogged and are able to stay in games effectively.

Jack Evans and Dean Campbell coming into the midfield add a grit that should mean Notts are not nice to play against. They'll be aggressive and tenacious and hopefully the team will be effective in its approach.

Weaknesses? To put it simply, where are the goals going to come from? Notts lost three of their four top scorers from last season over the summer, and rumours continue to circulate about where Jodi Jones' future lies. If he leaves, Notts will have lost 58 of their 74 league goals from last season, most notably Alassana Jatta.

Without signing a new out-and-out forward, they could really struggle to find the net regularly.

Difference-maker? All that being said above, they have a player in Cal Roberts who is able to provide a bit of magic from the number 10 position.

He's also never played as high as League One before, so it could be a risk to be dependent on him for those moments of class. But if he's able to do it at this level, he could help turn losses into draws and draws into wins and hopefully score the goals to keep Notts in League One by the end of the season.

## Oxford United

Getty Images

Former Wales international Aaron Ramsey replaced Matt Bloomfield as Oxford boss in June

Jerome Sale, BBC Radio Oxford

Manager: Aaron Ramsey

Last season: 22nd in the Championship, relegated

Key ins: None.

Key outs: Will Lankshear (loan ended), Myles Peart-Harris (loan ended), Przemysław Płacheta (Austin FC, free)

Expectations? The

arrival of Aaron Ramsey, external was taken as a signal that Oxford would be going all out for an instant return to the Championship. But even before his feet were under the desk, the club was placed under a temporary transfer embargo, expected to effectively be in place until January.

The aim will be to stay in contention until then. Four wins from four in pre-season, and with Ramsey getting Oxford back to playing a more energetic, positive brand of football, the mood has lightened a little. Predictions of how the U's will fare, understandably, vary wildly.

Strengths? Oxford have implemented a retention policy which means they've (so far) hung onto most of the contracted top talent, virtually all with Championship experience.

There have been some impressive performances in pre-season from young players who were being blocked last year by a bloated squad of senior players and the difficulties of blooding them in a relegation battle.

Weaknesses? Ramsey has to maintain harmony when at least some of the players he will wish to deploy might have expected their futures to lie elsewhere. He also has to hope that a lack of depth isn't exposed by any injury crisis, with Oxford looking vulnerable in a couple of positions.

Their only summer signing Frankie Kent (Hearts, free) will miss most of the season with a knee injury.

Difference-maker? Wales international Mark Harris scored 19 goals for Oxford in League One in 2023-24. Oxford need him to recapture that sort of form.

## Peterborough United

Ben Stevenson, BBC Radio Cambridgeshire

Manager: Luke Williams

Last season: 18th

Key ins: Thomas O'Connor (Wrexham, free), Evan Weir (Walsall, free), Owura Edwards (Colchester, free), Harrison Jones (Sunderland, undisclosed), Collin Andeng-Ndi (Southend, undisclosed)

Key outs: Archie Collins (released), Kyrell Lisbie (Millwall, undisclosed), Tom Lees (Harrogate, free), Cian Hayes (Rochdale, undisclosed), Abraham Odoh (Salford, undisclosed)

Expectations? The aim from the club's hierarchy is to challenge for promotion, but given Posh have finished 18th for the past two seasons and are restricted by the current financial position, many fans would be content with avoiding another flirt with relegation.

Strengths? It might be a cliche to say Posh's biggest asset right now is their manager Luke Williams, but there is more than an element of truth in that statement.

When he was appointed last October, he took a side bottom of the table and turned them into play-off contenders. Their promotion dreams were dashed when his young, injury hit squad hit a wall. The hope this season is that there are less injuries and Williams is able to recruit more players suited to his style of play.

Weaknesses? Having lost some of their best players from last season, some of the fanbase have been quick to bemoan the lack of proven quality coming in to replace them.

Injuries have undoubtably hampered Posh for the past couple of seasons, whilst a lack of experience and physical presence were also issues that haven't really been addressed yet.

Difference-maker? Peterborough United's two stand out players play at opposite ends of the pitch.

Goalkeeper Alex Bass is arguably the best goalkeeper the club has had for many years. Striker Harry Leonard has shown he has the potential to follow in the footsteps of some of the great forwards to have played at London Road in recent times. Both players will be key if Peterborough are to achieve their target.

## Plymouth Argyle

Manager: Tom Cleverley

Last season: Eighth

Key ins: Alex Mitchell (Charlton, undisclosed), Wes Harding (Millwall, free), Murphy Cooper (QPR, undisclosed)

Key outs: Lorent Tolaj (Bristol City, undisclosed)

## Reading

Tim Dellor, BBC Radio Berkshire

Manager: Leam Richardson

Last season: 12th

Key ins: Josh Stokes (Bristol City, loan), Udoka Godwin-Malife (Burton, undisclosed), Kyreece Lisbie (Colchester, undisclosed), Jacob Brown (Luton, free)

Key outs: Derrick Williams (retired), Matt Ritchie (retired), Kelvin Ehibhatiomhan (AC Horsens, undisclosed)

Expectations? Modest expectations right now. Reading are starting their fourth consecutive season in League One. With the squad as it is right now, in all probability they will be starting a fifth this time next year. The most optimistic fans will be hoping Reading sneak into the play-offs.

Strengths? Jack Marriott staying fit is central to success. If he plays more than 40 games, Reading will expect to finish in the top six. The issue is his fitness record in recent seasons. Getting more than 30 games from him will be good progress.

Lewis Wing in central midfield dictates everything, and is frequently head and shoulders above his team-mates, bringing real quality to games. Expect big seasons from Paudie O'Connor and Joel Pereira, as well. These experienced players are Championship quality.

Weaknesses? Right now there's a worrying lack of depth. The last few seasons has seen a string of injured players missing crucial games, and the club ought to fear the worst and hope for the best when it comes to planning for this campaign.

Right now a couple of injuries in defence and attack would leave the squad looking extremely flimsy. Recruiting a robust, athletic striker and central defender will cost money, but would make all the difference.

Difference-maker? This time last year, and the year before, it was Lewis Wing. It's the same answer this time, though Jack Marriott, if he can stay fit, is also a valid answer now.
